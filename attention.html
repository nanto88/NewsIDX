Not “viral”. Sectors gives headlines, outlets, tags and dates — no shares, no reads, no social signal. Nothing in the data measures virality, and a page that claimed to would be inventing it. What the data does measure is pickup: how many outlets ran one story, how fast, and whether that is unusual for that name. Everything below is built from that.

Trending topics · last 7 days against the rest of the month

Identical maths to “distinctive terms”, pointed at a 7-day window — so once that function exists this panel is a different date range and a different heading. One change from the month version: it counts tags, not n-grams. A week holds a handful of distinct stories, and a bigram seen twice against a bigram seen once separates nothing; tags are a small fixed vocabulary every story votes in, so a week is enough to move one. At fixture scale even this is underpowered — the numbers below are a layout, and the panel only earns its place at live volume.

What this cannot tell you

  • Reach. Five outlets is five outlets. It is not five hundred thousand readers, and the page never implies it.
  • Importance. A syndicated press release gets picked up hard by design. Pickup measures attention paid, not consequence.
  • Causation. When a price move is shown beside a story it is same-day co-occurrence, printed as such. The story did not “cause” the move, and the wording never says it did.
  • Completeness. Only outlets in the Sectors feed are counted, so pickup is a floor, never a total.
